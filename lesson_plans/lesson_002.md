# Lesson 2 Plan - Breakfast Or Lunch?

This extension-only planning card supports backfilling the extension practice
for Lesson 2. The main courseware was produced before per-lesson cards existed.

## Basic Info

- Lesson: 2
- Title: `Breakfast or lunch?`
- Chinese title: `早餐还是午餐？`
- Core bridge: `时间感错位带来的小幽默。`

## Extension Practice Direction

Lesson 2 extension production should be extension-only. Create
`web/data/extension/lesson_2.json` and register `2` in
`web/data/extension/index.json`. Do not regenerate script, audio, timeline,
read-aloud data, living-scene data, or storyboard images.

### Extension Reading

Use an original modern weekend story about a student waking very late and
misreading the time because of dark weather, closed curtains, or a phone left in
another room. Do not retell Aunt Lucy's phone call. Keep the humor gentle: the
student thinks it is morning, but the day has already moved on.

Suggested title: `A Very Late Morning`

Recommended word bank: `until`, `outside`, `rang`, `arrived`, `repeat`,
`lunchtime`, `looked out`, `dark`, `What a day`, `one o'clock`.

The 5 reading questions should test:

1. why the student thought it was still morning
2. what signal finally revealed the real time
3. what the student was doing at the wrong time
4. how another person reacted
5. the joke: breakfast language collides with lunchtime reality

### Sentence Writing

Use 4 patterns:

1. `I never ... on ...`
   - Source: `I never get up early on Sundays.`
   - Focus: a regular habit on a particular day.
   - Must include: `I never` and `on`
2. `I sometimes stay ... until ...`
   - Source: `I sometimes stay in bed until lunchtime.`
   - Focus: habit plus time limit.
   - Must include: `sometimes` and `until`
3. `Just then, ... rang`
   - Source: `Just then, the telephone rang.`
   - Focus: a sudden interruption in a story.
   - Must include: `Just then` and `rang`
4. `Do you always ... so ...?`
   - Source: `Do you always get up so late?`
   - Focus: a surprised question about someone's habit.
   - Must include: `Do you always` and `so`
