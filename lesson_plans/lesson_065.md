## Non-Negotiable Production Rules

This plan follows the current courseware presentation rule and the section
responsibility rule in `LESSON_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md`.

- Keep the opening hook short.
  - Use 4-6 spoken lines only.
  - The hook should ask: if someone wants to do a kind thing, does that mean
    they can use any method in a public street?
  - Do not retell the whole elephant-and-police story before the four `retell`
    frames.
- Treat the circus-and-elephant background as an older-life context.
  - Explain that old circuses often used animal performances.
  - Add a modern note that today we care much more about animal welfare and
    public safety.
  - Do not teach the child that riding an elephant through a city street is a
    charming thing to copy.
- Keep Jimmy Gates sympathetic but not automatically right.
  - He wants to take presents to a children's hospital.
  - His intention is kind.
  - His plan is unsafe and unsuitable for the main street.
  - The core lesson is: kindness still needs a safe and sensible plan.
- Explain the traffic logic clearly.
  - `main street` is the busy central road.
  - `side street` is less busy and would cause less disruption.
  - `holding up the traffic` means delaying or blocking vehicles and people.
- Make the humour gentle.
  - Jumbo is not naughty or dangerous; he is large, heavy, and refuses to move.
  - The police have a hard time, but they are amused.
  - The final joke about arresting Jumbo should land as friendly police humour,
    not real punishment.
- Story spine must be Chinese-first.
  - Present the event chain in Chinese first.
  - Attach short English chunks after each Chinese anchor.
  - Do not show an all-English plot chain as the main memory path.
- Avoid repeated script content across sections.
  - The four `retell` frames are the only section that should walk through the
    passage in order.
  - The teacher-student dialogue should deepen good intention versus public
    rules, animal welfare, and the humour of `let him off this time`.
  - Vocabulary and grammar sections should explain language tools, not retell
    the whole story again.
- Generated illustrations must be text-free.
  - No readable English or Chinese text inside images.
  - No readable hospital signs, street signs, banners, Christmas cards,
    police notices, speech bubbles, UI panels, or fake lesson text.
  - Render words such as `Father Christmas`, `main street`, `side street`,
    `holding up the traffic`, and `let him off` as real UI text outside the
    image.
- Keep script and image prompts separate.
  - Teaching script must not contain image prompt wording, camera directions,
    or generated-image mechanics.
  - Storyboard prompts must not leak into spoken lesson content.

## Production Warnings

- The old circus/elephant context needs modern handling.
  - Do not romanticize animal performances.
  - Do not show Jumbo being hurt, frightened, whipped, dragged, chained, or
    treated cruelly.
  - The scene can be comic, but animal welfare and city safety should remain in
    the background as a modern value.
- `Father Christmas` is the British-style name used in the passage.
  - Explain it as Santa Claus / 圣诞老人.
  - Keep the Christmas context warm but do not turn the lesson into a general
    Christmas-culture lecture.
- `six pretty girls` is old-fashioned wording.
  - Do not dwell on appearance.
  - In narration, explain `guard of honour` as a ceremonial group accompanying
    him, not as a beauty display.
- `versus` in the title is playful.
  - Explain that it is like `vs.` in games or sports.
  - The lesson is not about a real fight between Jumbo and the police.
- `ought to have gone` and `should have known` are central.
  - They are after-the-event judgments: what someone should have done, but did
    not do.
- Do not change shared lesson data unless separately asked.

## Lesson Positioning

Lesson 65 is a comic public-space story with a warm intention underneath. Last
Christmas, circus owner Jimmy Gates decides to take presents to a children's
hospital. He dresses up as Father Christmas and rides a baby elephant called
Jumbo down the main street. The event sounds cheerful, but the plan is not
suitable for a busy city road. A policeman tells Jimmy that he ought to have
gone along a side street because Jumbo is holding up the traffic. Jimmy agrees
to move, but Jumbo refuses. Fifteen policemen have to push hard to get Jumbo off
the main street. They have a difficult time, but they are amused. A policeman
jokes that Jumbo should be arrested, but because he has a good record, they will
let him off this time.

The courseware should make the student understand:

`圣诞节送礼物给儿童医院 -> Jimmy 打扮成 Father Christmas -> 骑着小象 Jumbo 走上 main street -> 好心但方式不合适 -> 警察提醒他应该走 side street -> Jumbo 挡住交通并拒绝移动 -> 十五个警察费力把它推走 -> 警察很辛苦但觉得好笑 -> 最后用“放它一马”的玩笑收尾`

The central bridge is:

`有爱心是好事，但在公共空间里，方式也要安全、合适、守规则。`

## One-Sentence Hook

Jimmy wants to take Christmas presents to a children's hospital, but riding a
baby elephant down a busy main street turns a kind idea into a traffic problem.

## Vivid Teaching Opening

The opening should set up the moral and comic tension quickly. It must be a
short hook, not a complete retelling.

Suggested teacher narration:

`如果你想给儿童医院送礼物，这当然是一件很温暖的事。`

`可是，如果你骑着一头小象走在城市主街上，会发生什么？`

`Jimmy Gates 的好心没有问题，可是他的方式出了问题。`

`Jumbo 太大、太重，还不肯走，结果警察也被卷进来了。`

`今天我们学一句很重要的话：kind idea, safe plan。`

Tone:

- Warm, comic, and gently corrective.
- Make Jimmy's intention kind, but his plan clearly unsuitable.
- Keep Jumbo lovable and calm.
- Save the full sequence for the four `retell` frames.

## Cultural And Background Bridge

The lesson needs a compact modern bridge:

1. `Father Christmas`
   - British-style wording for Santa Claus.
   - In the story, the costume makes Jimmy look festive and friendly.
2. `children's hospital`
   - This explains Jimmy's good intention.
   - The delivery of presents is warm and charitable.
3. Old circus background
   - Traditional circuses often used animal acts.
   - Modern teaching should mention that today people care more about animal
     welfare and would usually avoid such a street stunt.
4. Public-space safety
   - A busy main street belongs to everyone.
   - Even a kind event needs permission, a safe route, and traffic planning.
5. Police humour
   - The police are not cruel or angry in this story.
   - They have a difficult time but find the situation amusing.
   - `let him off this time` is a friendly joke.

## Teacher-Student Dialogue Direction

The dialogue should not retell the whole passage. It should deepen the modern
meaning.

Suggested dialogue spine:

1. Student asks whether Jimmy is a good person or a troublemaker.
   - Teacher: his intention is kind, but his plan is not safe for a main
     street.
2. Student asks why a baby elephant in the street is a problem.
   - Teacher: public roads need traffic rules; Jumbo is big, slow, and
     unpredictable.
3. Student asks about old circuses.
   - Teacher: old stories often show animal acts; today we pay more attention
     to animal welfare and would choose a safer method.
4. Student asks why the police are amused.
   - Teacher: the situation is difficult but not malicious; the final line is
     friendly humour.
5. Teacher connects to the student:
   - If you organize a charity event, think:
     `kind idea + safe route + permission + respect for others`.

The dialogue should leave the student feeling:

- I understand Jimmy's good intention.
- I know why the police could not allow it.
- I can see the old circus background without copying it today.
- I understand the joke in `let him off this time`.

## Grammar Handling

Do not start from a modal-verb table. Start from what people say after the event
has gone wrong.

### Job 1 - After-The-Event Judgment

Key lines:

- `He should have known that the police would never allow this sort of thing.`
- `he ought to have gone along a side street`

Teaching point:

These sentences look backward. They mean someone did not make the best choice.

Student explanation:

`事情已经发生了，大家回头看：Jimmy 本来应该知道警察不会允许；他本来应该走 side street。英语用 should have known, ought to have gone 来说“本来应该……但没有”。`

### Job 2 - Strong Guess From Evidence

Key line:

- `Jumbo must weigh a few tons.`

Teaching point:

`must` here is deduction, not command. The policeman is guessing strongly from
evidence: fifteen policemen had to push hard.

Student explanation:

`警察不是命令 Jumbo weigh a few tons，而是根据推不动它来判断：它肯定有好几吨重。`

### Job 3 - Lucky Non-Necessity

Key line:

- `it was fortunate that we didn't have to carry him`

Teaching point:

`didn't have to` means it was not necessary. It does not mean they tried and
failed.

Student explanation:

`幸好他们不用把 Jumbo 抬走。didn't have to carry him 是“不必抬它”，不是“没能抬它”。`

Use a compact display:

| Story meaning | English |
| --- | --- |
| He should have thought of it earlier | `should have known` |
| He should have used a side street | `ought to have gone` |
| I strongly guess Jumbo is very heavy | `must weigh a few tons` |
| Luckily, carrying was not necessary | `didn't have to carry him` |
| The police forgive the comic trouble | `let him off this time` |
